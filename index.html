What these sites are — read this first

Every site is built to be 100% compliant for Google Ads: a real business in a whitehat niche, with nothing on any page that a policy reviewer could call misrepresentation. Where a domain name suggested a restricted or licence-gated trade, the business was pivoted to the nearest safe niche that still fits the name — the reason is written on each card.

  • Nine pages each: home, three content pages, a conversion page with the form, privacy, terms, thanks and 404. CSS and JavaScript are inlined per page; the only outside request is the two Google Fonts families.
  • No fabricated proof anywhere. No testimonials, star ratings, customer counts, client logos, awards, certifications, founding years, founder names, registration numbers, prices or guarantees. The testimonial slot became an FAQ; the stats bar became a facts strip in words.
  • No phone number and no street address, deliberately: the business is placed by city and service area and reached through its mailbox and its form. Form-only conversion.
  • The cookie-consent banner is honest. It stores one preference in the visitor's browser and nothing else; no analytics or advertising tag is installed, and every privacy.html says so. If you add a tag later, the banner's toggle is where it hooks in — edit privacy in the same change.
  • Every photograph is committed in the package (WebP, under 50 KB each, open-licence, credited on the terms page). Nothing is hot-linked, so nothing can go missing later.
  • Identities from the domains' pasts were researched but NOT reused — no former owner's founder, address, phone or registration appears on any site. Structure and vocabulary only.

Read this before uploading anything

  1. Upload to any domain — nothing to set first. Canonical and Open Graph URLs are plain paths, and the form's redirect is filled in by the page itself from whatever address it is served on. The brand's own address is still written in two places on purpose: the mailbox the form posts to, and sitemap.xml plus the Sitemap: line in robots.txt. Edit those two by hand if the domain you use is a different one.
  2. Check the domain first. Seven of these ten domains currently carry live nameservers and several carry a working company mailbox setup (Microsoft 365, Zoho) — each card says what we found on 11 September. Two are unregistered. Confirm each one is yours before uploading; a site uploaded onto someone else's live domain is the one thing that cannot be undone quietly.
  3. Create the mailbox in cPanel, then submit that site's form once and click the activation email FormSubmit sends back. One activation per site — ten clicks in total. Until that is done, enquiries silently go nowhere.
  4. Stagger the launches and spread the hosting. These sites are deliberately unrelated to each other; putting all ten live on one IP on one day undoes that.
  5. Never cross-link them. No footer link, no "our other sites", nothing.
  6. If you add analytics, edit that site's privacy.html in the same change. All ten currently state truthfully that there is no analytics and there are no cookies.
  7. The 404 page is wired for Apache (.htaccess carries ErrorDocument 404 /404.html). On other servers point the error page at /404.html yourself.
