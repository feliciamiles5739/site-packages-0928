Ask about a piece

Ask about a piece, a size, or a floor.

One form, read by a person, answered from the room's own mailbox. Name a piece from its listing, or tell us the floor you want to cover, and the reply carries the note, the photographs and a plain answer on whether the piece is still here.

There is no shopping basket on this site and no price list on any page. Every piece is one piece, so every sale begins with a question, and this is where the question goes.

The form

What to put in the message

The name of the piece, if a listing gave you one. The floor you mean to cover, and the band of floor you want left showing. The colours already in the room, in your own words.

Asking about a cushion cover, say which insert you already own: covers are listed by the insert that fills them out.

What is not needed: a budget, a deadline, or a reason. Every piece is one piece and is quoted as itself, so a question costs nothing to ask and commits nothing on either side.

Nothing here is a hold. A piece is held only once the room has written back to say the window has started, and the window is stated in that reply rather than guessed at here.

What happens after sending

Your message lands in the room's mailbox and nowhere else. It is read on the next working day at the latest, Tuesday to Saturday, and answered by a person, from the same mailbox, to the address you gave.

If you named a piece, the reply says first whether it is still in the room. If it is, the note follows: size to the centimetre, knot count, pile height, fibre and wear, with further photographs in daylight, close views of the pile and of the back, and the ends and edges in frame. If it has gone, the reply says so, and offers nothing in its place unless you asked for something like it.

If you sent a floor, the reply lists the pieces in the room that fit inside it, each with its note. If you asked a question the site has not answered, you get an answer, and if the answer is that the room does not do the thing, that is said plainly rather than passed to somebody who does.

Nothing is held and nothing is invoiced until you write back to say you want a piece. The form is a question, not an order.

The days the mailbox is read

Tuesday to Saturday, from ten in the morning until six in the evening. A message sent on Sunday or Monday waits until Tuesday, and nothing about it is lost by waiting.

No acknowledgement lands on its own; the first thing you get back is written by a person. If a week has gone by, the message did not arrive, and it is worth sending again.

The room, and where pieces go

The mailbox

studio@azitamehrabi.com

Writing to it directly does the same as the form. Put the piece's name in the subject line and it is found faster.

The room

Toronto, Ontario. Pieces are measured and photographed here, and a time to see one in person is arranged by writing. There is no walk-in shop and no counter to turn up at.

Where pieces travel

Anywhere in Canada, rolled and wrapped, by courier. Not outside Canada, and not fitted or laid at the other end.
