030

How an order runs

From the first email to the box on your bench, in the order it happens.

01 · The list

Send a part list by email or through the request page. A good list has one line per item: the family, the form, the capacity or size, and the quantity. If you know the maker’s part number, add it; if you only know what the item looks like, describe it and we will match it. A list of a single beaker and a list of two hundred lines are handled the same way, by the same person, in the order they arrive.

02 · The reading

We read every line against what is on the shelf. Where a size is not stocked, the reply says so and offers the nearest capacity or the lead time to bring it in. Where a tolerance class is not stated, we quote Class B and mark the line so you can change it. Nothing is substituted without a note beside it.

03 · The price list

The reply is a priced copy of your list, by email, with a lead time on every line and the box quantity where an item is packed in tens or dozens. It is an offer that stands for the period written on it; it is not an invoice, and nothing is reserved until you confirm.

04 · Confirmation

You confirm by email. Only then does stock move. Split deliveries are possible when part of a list is on the shelf and part is on order; we say which lines fall in which delivery before you agree to it, never afterwards.

05 · Packing and dispatch

Glass is packed in dividers, never loose, and racks and stands travel with their rods and bases separated so nothing bends in the box. A packing list rides in the parcel and a copy goes by email so you can check the box against the list line by line.

06 · Hand-over

Count the box against the packing list. Breakage in transit is reported by email with the line and the number of pieces, and the line is re-sent. Wrong items go back and the right ones follow; nobody is asked to keep what they did not order.

What we value

Plain descriptions, printed figures, and a reply that says exactly what will arrive and when. We would rather tell you an item is not stocked than send the next size up without asking, and we would rather quote a box than pretend a box can be broken.

What we turn down

Requests for reagents, solvents or any chemical; requests for a calibration or a certificate; anything for medical, diagnostic, clinical or veterinary use; repairs to broken glass; and any judgement on whether an assembly or a method is safe for your work, which belongs with the people responsible for your bench, not with a supplier of glass.

040

Questions

The seven that arrive most often, answered as they are answered by email.

Do you supply chemicals or reagents?

No. Nothing that goes into the glass is stocked or sourced here: no reagents, no solvents, no reference materials, no controlled substances. If a list arrives with a chemical on it, that line is struck and the rest is quoted.

Are these items calibrated?

No. Volumetric glass carries the tolerance class and figure the maker printed on it, and that is what we repeat. We do not check it, adjust it or issue a certificate for it. If your work needs a certificate, it has to come from a laboratory equipped to issue one.

Can I order a single beaker?

Yes. A list of one line is still a list. Items the glassworks or the mill packs in box quantities are quoted by the box where breaking the box is not practical, and the reply says so on that line.

What tolerance class is this glassware?

The class printed on the piece: Class A or Class B for volumetric flasks, cylinders and pipettes. Beakers, conical flasks and funnels have no tolerance class; their scale is for indication only. The glassware page explains how the classes are read.

Do you sell to schools?

Yes, to teaching labs and workshops alike. The same rule applies: glass, racks and consumables only, and we cannot advise on what a teaching bench should hold; that is for the person who runs it.

Do you ship outside Belgium?

Within Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg as a matter of routine, and to neighbouring countries by agreement on the price list. Further than that we decline, because glass does not travel well and a broken box helps nobody.

Can you advise which item I need?

We can tell you what an item is, what capacities it comes in and which stopper or clamp fits it. We cannot tell you whether it is right for your method, your sample or your safety rules. Describe the item and we match it; the choice stays yours.
